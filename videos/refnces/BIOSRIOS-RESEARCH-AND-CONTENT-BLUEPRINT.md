- 25% evergreen courses and workflows

## 4. Reference lessons worth transferring

### Long-form YouTube

#### Dan Martell

Transfer:

- Compressed mastery promise
- One expressive host
- Thumbnail adds emotion instead of repeating the title

Do not transfer:

- Unsupported precision such as `97%`
- Shock copy without a matching payoff

#### AI education channels

Transfer:

- Repeatable series labels: `101`, `Full Setup`, `Tool Stack`, `Essential Skills`
- One host plus one workflow artifact
- Consistent shelf design across different topics

#### Nick Saraev

Transfer:

- Full-course authority
- Visible curriculum or workflow map
- Occasional exhaustive evergreen video

Do not make every upload a two-hour course. Flagship courses should be occasional search assets.

#### BridgeMind

Transfer:

- Fast coverage of named models and versions
- One job-to-be-done per release
- Consistent dark visual system

Do not publish a release video unless it teaches a durable workflow or changes a real task.

#### Sandy Lee AI

Transfer:

- Completed outcome first
- Finished website, workflow, edit, or deliverable as proof
- Clear experiment framing

#### AI trading channels

Transfer:

- Controlled comparisons
- Visible scoreboards
- One dramatic result

Do not transfer:

- Money claims without primary evidence
- Fake urgency
- Screens full of logos, arrows, and numbers

#### Fireship

Transfer the story spine:

1. Reversal hook
2. Stakes
3. Mystery
4. Reveal
5. Mechanism
6. Two escalating examples
7. Implication

Use primary-source excerpts, memes, diagrams, and real interfaces to maintain visual change through technical material.

### Short-form video

Use a warm cream/peach editorial field for hooks and explanations, then switch
to near-black proof cards for receipts. Use one original tactile object per
Short, the BiosRios character only at trust and reaction beats, and one direct
comment keyword after the lesson is complete.

The strongest transferable short-form formats are:

1. **Proof demo with host anchor**
   - Real output in the upper two-thirds
   - BiosRios character in the lower third
   - Large captions with 3 to 6 words
   - Best default format

2. **Mascot news explainer**
   - Code or source material in the background
   - Character reactions
   - Provocative hook followed by evidence

3. **Clean motion explainer**
   - Off-white background
   - Real screenshots
   - One idea per scene
   - Character appears at transitions, not as decoration

4. **Visual timeline**
   - Years, product versions, or model progression
   - Logos accumulate as the story progresses
   - Best for history and ecosystem changes

Avoid motivational footage unless it tells a specific BiosRios founder story. It can build identity, but it does not teach the niche.

## 5. The BiosRios packaging contract

Packaging is locked before the script.

Each concept gets three title and thumbnail pairs. The selected pair defines the first 20 seconds.

### Title rules

- Name the task, test, or finished outcome.
- Use the exact product only when it helps the viewer understand the job.
- Prefer a concrete verb: built, tested, fixed, replaced, ranked, caught.
- Do not use `insane`, `game-changing`, `10x`, or `only` as a substitute for a result.
- Do not claim numbers until the test produces them.

### Thumbnail rules

- Maximum four words.
- One tension, not a summary.
- One BiosRios character expression.
- One proof artifact: scorecard, terminal result, finished build, source excerpt, or before-and-after.
- Black, white, and Claude orange are the default palette.
- The character occupies roughly 30% to 40% of the frame.
- The artifact must remain readable on a small phone screen.
- The thumbnail must add information that is not already in the title.

### Opening rules

Every long-form opening follows this contract:

- 0 to 3 seconds: show the result, failure, or surprising artifact.
- 3 to 8 seconds: explain why the result matters.
- 8 to 15 seconds: state the test rules.
- 15 to 20 seconds: open one question the video will answer.

No logo animation. No greeting. No unrelated history before the click promise.

## 6. Long-form format

Default length: 6 to 12 minutes.

Occasional evergreen course: 18 to 35 minutes.

### Proof-first episode structure

1. Cold result
2. Test question
3. Fixed rules and scoring
4. First attempt
5. Failure or surprise
6. Second attempt or comparison
7. Evidence review
8. Reusable lesson
9. Honest limitation
10. One next-step call to action

### Visual rhythm

- Change the visual idea every 3 to 7 seconds.
- Hold longer only when the viewer must read evidence.
- Prefer real screens over decorative stock footage.
- Crop screens tightly around the active proof.
- Use the BiosRios character as narrator, judge, and reaction, not as constant wallpaper.
- Bring the character back at every structural transition.

## 7. Short-form format

Default length: 35 to 65 seconds.

### Short structure

- 0 to 1 second: contradiction or result
- 1 to 5 seconds: show proof
- 5 to 25 seconds: explain the mechanism
- 25 to 45 seconds: demonstrate or compare
- 45 to 55 seconds: state the reusable lesson
- Final beat: one natural next step, only when necessary

### Short visual system

- Real artifact in the upper two-thirds
- BiosRios character in the lower third or at scene transitions
- Captions in short phrases
- One emphasized word at a time
- No unrelated B-roll
- No generic `follow for more` ending

Each long-form research pack should yield:

- One opening-result Short
- One failure or surprise Reel
- One reusable lesson Short

## 8. Research and experiment protocol

Every proof-first video must be reproducible.

### Before the run

1. Define one task.
2. Freeze the input, repo state, files, and tool versions.
3. Write the scoring rubric before seeing the result.
4. Give each agent the same authority and constraints.
5. Record cost, time, interventions, errors, and final output.

### Suggested scorecard

| Category | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Completed the requested outcome | 35 |
| Correctness and regressions | 25 |
| Verification quality | 15 |
| Scope discipline | 10 |
| Time and cost | 10 |
| Clarity of handoff | 5 |

### After the run

1. Verify the output independently.
2. Save the exact surprising moments.
3. Choose the title from the actual result.
4. Build the thumbnail around the most legible proof.
5. Rewrite the first 20 seconds to pay off that exact proof.

Never decide the winner before the experiment.

## 10. Instagram and YouTube relationship

Instagram is not a separate research operation. It is the short-form testing surface for the same ideas.

Workflow:

1. Research one strong long-form question.
2. Test three short hooks before or around the long-form release.
3. Keep the hook that earns the strongest retention and saves.
4. Use comments as research questions for the long video or follow-up.
5. Do not paste the same caption or edit to every platform without adapting it.

The visual language remains consistent:

- Same character
- Same orange, black, and white palette
- Same proof artifact
- Different pacing and framing per platform

## 11. Measurement

### Public signals

- Views at 24 hours and 7 days
- Likes and comments
- Subscriber count
- Search ranking for the target query

### YouTube Studio signals

- Impressions
- Click-through rate
- First 30-second retention
- Average view duration
- Traffic source
- Subscribers gained per video

### Instagram signals

- 3-second hold
- Average watch time
- Completion rate
- Replays
- Saves
- Shares
- Profile visits
- Follows from the Reel

Public views alone cannot tell whether packaging or retention failed.

Decision tree:

- Low impressions: topic or channel distribution problem
- Normal impressions plus low click-through rate: title and thumbnail problem
- Good click-through rate plus early drop: opening promise problem
- Good opening plus middle drop: structure or density problem
- Good retention plus weak follows: unclear channel promise or weak next step

## 12. Production requirements for the clone

### Voice

- Use the existing cloned BiosRios voice.
- Generate multiple takes for unclear lines.
- Transcribe generated audio back and reject incorrect words.
- Rewrite sentences that repeatedly fail.
- Keep the delivery slightly rough and human, not announcer-perfect.

### Character

- Use the existing BiosRios character library.
- Select expressions from the script beat, not randomly.
- Use character motion to mark transitions or judgment.
- Do not let the character cover proof.
- Thumbnail character and opening character must match the video's emotional promise.

### Evidence

- Use real product screens.
- Use official logos.
- Use primary-source excerpts.
- Use actual test output.
- Blur private data.
- Never draw a fake interface when a real screenshot exists.
